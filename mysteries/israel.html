You Are Not Commanded by God to Blindly Follow Israel as a Christian

🕰️ Revised: Nov 2026

By Jake Rogers · Send me feedback

Many people recognize the word “Israel” from the Bible without knowing the whole story behind it. The Scriptures tell of God’s dealings with the Israelites in a particular covenant story. That story does not make a modern state, government, or political movement automatically favored by God—or immune from moral judgment.

I love my Jewish brothers and sisters. Jesus, my Lord and Savior, was Jewish. My criticism here is not of Jewish people. I am challenging political Zionism where it is used to excuse dispossession, occupation, or violence, and I am challenging Christian teaching that treats support for a modern government as a command from God. My aim is not hatred. I want people to stop dying.

Read the Bible—read the whole story

The Bible tells of God leading the Israelites out of Egypt and through a particular history involving covenant, land, obedience, failure, judgment, and hope. Its accounts of the conquest of Canaan are part of that ancient scriptural story. Christians disagree about how to interpret those difficult passages, including the judgment described there. But they do not give a modern leader a blank cheque to claim divine permission for war, displacement, or killing.

When I read the New Testament, I see Jesus calling his followers to love their enemies, make peace, and care for the vulnerable. I do not believe God is telling Benjamin Netanyahu to bomb Gaza, Lebanon, or Iran as he spoke to Moses or Joshua in the biblical narrative. A modern government’s claim—or a Christian’s claim—of divine favor still has to be tested against the teachings of Christ.

For me, Christian Zionism is an oxymoron: following Christ means judging political power by the way it treats human beings, not giving a flag automatic religious approval. That is my Christian conviction. Other Christians interpret the relevant passages differently, but no interpretation should make civilian suffering invisible.

A biblical name on a map is not proof that every later government acting in that place speaks for God.

Be precise about who is being criticized

“Israel” can mean a people, a state, a government, or a military. Those are not interchangeable. Here, when I criticize Zionism, I mean the political movement and the specific state policies and military actions discussed below—not Jewish people as a whole. Jewish people hold diverse beliefs and political views; no government, lobby, or movement speaks for every Jew.

The New Testament itself names particular actors in the accounts of Jesus’ death. Some leaders opposed him; Jesus was sentenced under the Roman prefect Pontius Pilate and executed by Roman authority. Those passages should not be turned into collective guilt assigned to Jewish people across time. Jesus and his earliest followers were Jewish, and the Gospel accounts also describe crowds welcoming him.

The word “chosen” in the Bible belongs to a complex scriptural and theological story. It should not be flattened into “God likes one ethnicity best” or used as a shield against criticism of a modern state. The Bible’s covenant language includes responsibility and judgment as well as promise.

The human cost in Gaza

UN Human Rights–OPT reported in October 2026 that Gaza’s Ministry of Health had reported more than 1,400 Palestinians killed in the year after the ceasefire announcement of 10 October 2025. By the end of July 2026, UN Human Rights–OPT had independently verified 1,076 conflict-related deaths during that period, including 268 children and 126 women. The UN says its verified total is not complete.

The same UN report says that, according to Gaza’s Ministry of Health, more than 20,000 children had been killed since 7 October 2023. OCHA’s 22 October 2026 report said eight children were reported killed and 20 injured in six days. It also described food prices rising, monthly food aid reaching fewer than one million people out of an estimated 2.1 million, and serious shortages affecting shelter, water, and medical care.

OCHA’s 29 October situation report recorded 33 Palestinians reported killed and 188 injured between 20 and 26 October, according to Gaza’s Ministry of Health. OCHA also documented continuing airstrikes, shelling, shooting, naval fire, destruction, and displacement. These are attributed reports, not a claim that every individual case has been independently verified by the UN.

A UN Commission of Inquiry has described Israel’s actions in Gaza as genocide; Israel rejects that finding. If we use that term, we should identify the commission and distinguish its finding from a final judgment by an international court. The legal debate does not erase the documented deaths, injuries, displacement, and destruction—or the urgency of protecting civilians.

If your heart cheers when bombs fall on children, is that the Spirit of Christ—or the adversary? “Love your enemies.” — Matthew 5:44. “Blessed are the peacemakers.” — Matthew 5:9.

Lebanon: displacement and the Ali al-Taher Ridge explosion

A UN News report in October 2026 said more than 300,000 people displaced since early March remained internally displaced in Lebanon. Nearly 970,000 people had returned to communities in the south, but many faced damaged homes, disrupted essential services, and difficulty making a living. The UN also reported further demolitions, fires, and Israeli strikes during that week, both in and beyond an Israeli-declared combat zone.

On 10 September 2026, Israel said it destroyed two Hezbollah tunnels beneath the Ali al-Taher Ridge in southern Lebanon using 1,100 tonnes of explosives. The National reported that the blast registered as a magnitude 4.1 tremor and that residents described homes shaking and windows breaking. Israel said the underground complex contained Hezbollah military infrastructure; that description should be attributed to Israel. The scale of the explosion and its effects on nearby communities deserve scrutiny in their own right.

The same report quoted a regional analyst who described Israeli forces occupying a buffer zone, demolishing villages and infrastructure, and making the area uninhabitable. That is the analyst’s assessment, not an independently verified UN finding. It should be presented with that attribution.

Iran, U.S. military costs, and whose interests are served?

The United States and Israel launched strikes on Iran on 28 February 2026, according to the Congressional Research Service’s account of the conflict. Israel has described its security rationale; U.S. officials have given their own stated reasons for U.S. military action. I believe the campaign aligned with Israeli strategic interests, and that alignment deserves public scrutiny. Alignment alone does not prove that the United States went to war because Israel wanted it to.

A Pentagon estimate reported by Stars and Stripes put U.S. military costs at $47.1 billion through 16 October 2026. That is an estimate of U.S. military operations—not money handed to Israel. The reported estimate included operating costs, replacement munitions, and lost equipment. Readers should be able to distinguish the cost of America’s own war from U.S. military assistance to Israel.

Because this essay’s map is limited to the specific Gaza and Lebanon incidents sourced above, it does not plot Iran without a precise, sourced location and event. The distinction matters: a useful map should document what happened, where, when, and according to whom.

Political machinery and public money

The United States has pledged $38 billion in military assistance to Israel for fiscal years 2019–2028, subject to congressional appropriations, according to the Congressional Research Service. That long-term assistance is separate from the estimated cost of U.S. military operations in the Iran war.

AIPAC is a pro-Israel lobbying organization. Its affiliated United Democracy Project is a super PAC that spends in U.S. elections. Their lobbying, election spending, public filings, contributors, and policy positions can be examined directly. Name the organization and cite the records; do not turn documented political influence into a claim about Jewish people collectively.

Criticism of particular government policies should not automatically be treated as hatred of a people. At the same time, accusations about lobbying, donors, or media influence need named actors, public evidence, and careful wording. Accountability is strongest when it is specific.

Criticism, antisemitism, and speech

There is a real debate about whether institutions sometimes apply antisemitism policies in ways that chill criticism of Israel. The IHRA working definition itself says that criticism of Israel comparable to criticism of any other country is not antisemitic; its examples and their institutional application are contested. That distinction matters. Criticizing a government or Zionist political movement is not inherently antisemitic, while anti-Jewish prejudice must be named and rejected wherever it appears.

Universities, governments, and platforms should protect people from discrimination without treating every criticism of state policy as bigotry. And critics should be specific about the policy, institution, or official they mean.

The Biblical Warning

“O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, you who kill the prophets and stone those sent to you.” — Matthew 23:37

“They will not leave one stone on another, because you did not recognize the time of God’s coming to you.” — Luke 19:44

“Come out of her, My people, so that you will not share in her sins.” — Revelation 18:4

These passages speak within their scriptural contexts. Applying them to a modern state or political movement is a Christian interpretation, not an explicit naming of that modern entity in the text.

The Call to Christians

Christian, you are not commanded to blindly follow Israel. You are commanded to follow Christ.

Christ does not bless bombs.
Christ does not delight in suffering.
Christ does not excuse cruelty.

Blind loyalty to a flag is idolatry. Loyalty to Christ is freedom. Pick your side: Christ or Babylon.
